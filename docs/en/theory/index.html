Theory

PLC programming theory: Ladder (LAD) and STL explained with examples

Everything you need to program PLCs in Ladder (LAD, called KOP in German) and Statement List (STL, called AWL in German), topic by topic and with worked examples you can try right here. It is the theory built into the Automatizacion simulator, whose interface is in Spanish.

  1. 01 What is a PLC Introduction to programmable logic controllers: what a PLC is, its parts, how it differs from relays and from an Arduino, which programming languages exist (Ladder, FBD, STL, structured text and GRAFCET) and which one to learn first.
  2. 02 The scan cycle How a programmable logic controller runs its program: it reads the inputs, executes OB1 and writes the outputs. What this means for your Ladder and STL programs, and why a normally closed stop button is programmed with a normally open contact.
  3. 03 Memory areas The memory areas of a PLC (I, Q, M, T, C, DB, L and peripheral I/O), how bits, bytes, words and double words are addressed, and what bit memory is used for.
  4. 04 Data types Ladder and STL data types with their ranges and constants: BOOL, BYTE, WORD, INT, DINT, REAL, S5TIME and TIME. How BCD works, the internal format of S5TIME and symbolic addressing.
  5. 05 Blocks: OB, FC, FB and DB How a PLC program is structured in blocks: organization blocks (OB), functions (FC), function blocks (FB) with their instance DB, and data blocks (DB). The most important OBs and how blocks are called in STL.
  6. 06 Ladder: contacts and coils How to read and program a Ladder diagram (LAD, called KOP in German), step by step: normally open and normally closed contacts, contacts in series (AND) and in parallel (OR), coils, Set, Reset and NOT, with examples you can try right here and their STL equivalent.
  7. 07 STL: RLO and bit logic The STL (AWL) logic instructions explained one by one: A, AN, O, ON, X, XN, =, S, R and NOT, how the RLO is calculated, how to group with brackets and how to solve an exercise by hand, with examples you can try right here.
  8. 08 Latching, Set and Reset How to program a start/stop circuit in Ladder and STL: latching (self-holding), Set and Reset coils, SR and RS flip-flops, and how the priority between start and stop is decided.
  9. 09 Edge detection What a rising or falling edge is, how it is detected in Ladder with (P) and (N) and in STL with FP and FN, why each edge needs its own memory bit, and how to build a toggle (impulse) relay.
  10. 10 Timers The five Ladder and STL timers explained with timing diagrams: pulse (SP), extended pulse (SE), on-delay (SD), retentive on-delay (SS) and off-delay (SF). With worked examples.
  11. 11 Counters Up and down counters in Ladder and STL: pins, loading the preset value in BCD, reset, and reading the value in binary or in BCD. With a car park example.
  12. 12 Clock memory What a PLC’s clock memory is, how to enable it, the frequency of each bit and how to use it to make a lamp flash or repeat an action every second, with Ladder and STL examples.
  13. 13 Sequences and GRAFCET How to program a sequence in a PLC: what a GRAFCET is, how to turn it into Ladder and STL with step bits and Set/Reset, and the complete example of a traffic light that you can try.
  14. 14 Compare and arithmetic How to work with numbers in a PLC: load and transfer with L and T, compare integers and reals, add, subtract, multiply and divide, and what happens to the status bits on overflow.
  15. 15 Conversions How to convert between BCD, INT, DINT and REAL in STL and Ladder, how to round, how to scale an analog input to a percentage, and how to mask and shift bits with word logic.
  16. 16 Jumps and loops Conditional and unconditional jumps in STL (JU, JC, JCN, JZ…), their Ladder equivalent (JMP, JMPN) and loops with LOOP, with the pitfalls that come up most often in exams.
  17. 17 Indirect addressing Memory-indirect and register-indirect addressing in STL, the area pointer format and the Master Control Relay (MCR), with an example that adds up a table of words.
  18. 18 Ladder ↔ STL How to translate a Ladder network into STL and back: each element with its instruction, series and parallel patterns, and how boxes with EN/ENO are translated. With worked exercises.
  19. 19 Solved exercises Typical PLC exercises solved in Ladder and STL and checked in the simulator: filling a tank with hysteresis, a staircase light timer, a warning every 5 parts and a star-delta motor start.
  20. 20 Mnemonics and glossary Table of equivalences between the German (U, E, A, SPB…) and the English (A, I, Q, JC…) STL mnemonics, and a glossary of Ladder and STL terms.

Practice with the simulator
