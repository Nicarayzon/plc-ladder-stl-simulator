Load, compare and arithmetic in Ladder and STL

How to work with numbers in a PLC: load and transfer with L and T, compare integers and reals, add, subtract, multiply and divide, and what happens to the status bits on overflow.

Boxes with EN/ENO in Ladder

Comparison (they act like a contact: they are placed in series or in parallel):

FamilyBoxes
Compare INTCMP ==I, <>I, >I, <I, >=I, <=I
Compare DINT / REALthe same with D / R
TransferMOVE (IN → OUT, 8, 16 or 32 bits)
INT arithmeticADD_I, SUB_I, MUL_I, DIV_I
DINT arithmeticADD_DI, SUB_DI, MUL_DI, DIV_DI, MOD_DI
REAL arithmeticADD_R, SUB_R, MUL_R, DIV_R, ABS, SQR, SQRT, EXP, LN, SIN, COS, TAN, ASIN, ACOS, ATAN
ConversionBCD_I, I_BCD, BCD_DI, DI_BCD, I_DI, DI_R, ROUND, TRUNC, CEIL, FLOOR, INV_I, INV_DI, NEG_I, NEG_DI, NEG_R
Word logicWAND_W, WOR_W, WXOR_W, WAND_DW, WOR_DW, WXOR_DW

  • ENO = 0 if EN = 0 or if there was an error. For example, ADD_I with overflow or I_BCD outside ±999.
  • Boxes can be chained: the ENO of one → the EN of the next.
  • There is no I_R: to go from INT to REAL you have to use I_DI and then DI_R.

Load and transfer

InstructionEffect
L opACCU2 := ACCU1; ACCU1 := op. A byte or a word is loaded into the low part of ACCU1 and the rest is filled with zeros (without sign extension)
T opop := low part of ACCU1 (1, 2 or 4 bytes depending on the operand). It is always executed, whatever the RLO is. It does not change the accumulators
LC T n / LC C nLoads the value of a timer or counter in BCD
L STW / T STWReads or writes the status word
L DBNO / L DBLGNumber / length of the open DB
      L     MW 10           // ACCU1 = MW10             ACCU2 = (previous)
      L     MW 12           // ACCU1 = MW12             ACCU2 = MW10
      +I                    // ACCU1 = MW10 + MW12      ACCU2 = MW10 (unchanged with 2 ACCUs)
      T     MW 14           // MW14 := ACCU1-L

T is unconditional. To transfer only when a condition is met, you have to jump over it: A I0.0 / JCN NO / L 5 / T MW10 / NO: NOP 0.

Zeros, not the sign. L MW 10 with MW10 = −1 (W#16#FFFF) leaves ACCU1 = DW#16#0000FFFF. To treat it as a DINT you have to use ITD.

  • With 2 accumulators, ACCU2 does not change after the operation.
  • Integer division truncates toward 0: -17 /I 5 gives quotient −3 and remainder −2.

Example: 30000 + 10000 → −25536 is stored, with OV = 1 and OS = 1.

  • Division by 0: CC1 = CC0 = 1, OV = OS = 1. The result is not valid.
  • REAL: an infinite result sets OV and OS to 1. An invalid result (NaN) also sets CC1 = CC0 = 1.

Comparison

==I, <>I, >I, <I, >=I, <=I compare ACCU2 with ACCU1: L a / L b / >I means "a > b". The …D variants compare DINTs and the …R variants compare REALs.

  • The result (1 or 0) is written to the RLO and replaces the previous one: it is not combined with it. To use it in series with contacts it has to go inside brackets:
      A     I 2.0
      A(
      L     MW 30
      L     500
      >I
      )
      =     Q 5.6           // Q5.6 = I2.0 ∧ (MW30 > 500)
  • The comparison also sets CC1/CC0 (see STL: RLO and bit logic: CPU registers), sets OV = 0 and leaves /FC = 1. The chain can carry on with A, O...

Worked examples

E20Level comparison

Task: MW30 = level in litres.

  • Q5.4 = high level (> 500).
  • Q5.5 = low level (< 100).
  • Q5.6 = high-level alarm, only if I2.0 (enable) = 1.

Concepts: the comparison replaces the RLO, so to combine it with a contact it goes inside brackets.

      L     MW 30
      L     500
      >I
      =     Q 5.4
      L     MW 30
      L     100
      <I
      =     Q 5.5
      A     I 2.0
      A(
      L     MW 30
      L     500
      >I
      )
      =     Q 5.6

E21Addition with overflow detection

Task: MW44 = MW40 + MW42. M1.0 = this addition overflowed. M1.1 = overflow memory (cleared with I0.0).

      L     MW 40
      L     MW 42
      +I
      T     MW 44
      A     OV
      =     M 1.0
      A     OS
      S     M 1.1
      A     I 0.0
      R     M 1.1

E22Integer multiplication and division (quotient and remainder)

Concepts: *I leaves a DINT in ACCU1. /I leaves the quotient in ACCU1-L and the remainder in ACCU1-H.

      L     MW 50
      L     MW 52
      *I
      T     MD 54       // full product (32 bits)
      L     MW 50
      L     MW 52
      /I
      T     MW 58       // quotient
      SRD   16          // brings the remainder (ACCU1-H) to the low part
      T     MW 60       // remainder

Test yourself

Work out the answer before you open the solution.

Q03Does T depend on the RLO?

With I0.0 = 0, what are the values of MW30 and Q4.0?

      A     I 0.0
      L     5
      T     MW 30
      =     Q 4.0
Solution

MW30 = 5 (T is unconditional) and Q4.0 = 0 (L and T do not touch the RLO).

Frequently asked questions

What happens if an INT addition goes out of range?

The result wraps around: 30000 + 10000 leaves −25536 in the accumulator and the overflow bits OV and OS are set. The CPU does not stop.

Does the T instruction depend on the RLO?

No. L and T are always executed, whatever the RLO is. To transfer only when a condition is met, you have to jump over it with JCN.

What does /I leave in the accumulator?

The quotient in the low part (ACCU1-L) and the remainder in the high part (ACCU1-H). For example, L 17 / L 5 / /I leaves 3 at the bottom and 2 at the top.

Why does a comparison in series with a contact go inside brackets?

Because the comparison replaces the RLO instead of combining with it. Inside A( … ) it is calculated separately and then combined with the contact.
